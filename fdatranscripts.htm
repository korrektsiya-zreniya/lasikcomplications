Editor's note: This device causes a loss of endothelial cells, which are vital for the health of the cornea. These cells do not regenerate. If too many cells are lost, the cornea will become cloudy and a corneal transplant may be required. In the discussion that follows, panel members were concerned about the potential long-term decline of endothelial cell density with this device. Dr. Rosenthal, FDA Division Director, speaks up to protect physicians at the expense of patients' well-being. More from the transcript:

Editor's note: Pupil size measurement was required in the guidance document for clinical trials of lasers for LASIK. So why did the FDA not require the investigators follow the protocol? FDA approved this device based on insufficient information. Subsequently, many patients with large pupils were treated on this technology and suffered permanent visual impairment.
